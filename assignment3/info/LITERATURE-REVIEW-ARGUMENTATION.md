# Literature Review Paper - Argumentation Guide

A literature review paper provides a comprehensive overview of the literature in a specific area and critically evaluates relevant publications. It is an argumentative text.

## Aim

Synthesize relevant literature and add value by coming to new and interesting conclusions. A literature review is more than just a summary — it critically compares and contrasts findings to come to new insights.

## Two Types

1. **Comparative literature review**: describes several theories/methods/etc. and adds value by comparing them based on evaluation criteria.
2. **Explorative literature review**: describes the state-of-the-art and adds value by exploring the most important gaps that remain.

---

## Argumentation by Section

### Introduction

Aim: convince the reader of the importance of your research. Must contain motive, objective, and structure overview.

**Motive** answers:
- What is the problem in society or science?
- What has already been researched?
- What do we not know yet? (scientific gap)
- Why is it important that this gap will be filled? (relevance)

The objective should follow logically from these four arguments (dependent argumentation — all are required).

### Method

Aim: show how you conducted research and convince the reader it was well executed (reliable answer to RQ).

- Justify search terms and databases if not obvious
- Justify selection criteria (year, language, citations)
- For comparative reviews: explain and justify evaluation criteria

### Results

Aim: outline findings that provide a convincing answer to the research question. Results are the arguments (evidence) that justify the standpoint (conclusion).

#### Comparative literature reviews

- Subconclusion for each evaluation criterion
- Together these support the main conclusion (dependent argumentation)
- If results don't all point in the same direction, argue why one criterion is more important than another
- All findings must be supported with references (an argumentative move)

#### Explorative literature reviews

Three steps:
1. Describe the state-of-the-art (what has been researched)
2. Describe what is currently unknown (from critically comparing/contrasting findings)
3. Argue why researching the unknown is worth time and effort

Similar to introduction argumentation but more concrete — specific developments, specific gaps, specific reasons.

### Discussion

Aim: answer research question and critically reflect on the study.

- Restate RQ and answer it (conclusion)
- Summarize main results (evidence justifying conclusion)
- Explain implications (what results mean in context of existing literature)
- Discuss limitations (counterarguments against validity/trustworthiness)
- Refute counterarguments to show outcomes are still valid
