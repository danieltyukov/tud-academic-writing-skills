# Scientific Argumentation Model (SAM)

Scientific texts using IMRD structure contain argumentative elements:

## Argumentative Elements

| Element | Found in | Function | Signal words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motive** | Introduction | Why is the research important? (unknown, conflicting results, problem needing solution) | "is still unknown", "need to be studied" |
| **Objective** | Introduction | What do the authors want to know? (research aim, question, hypothesis, design aim) | "we will study", "we aim to" |
| **Main conclusion** | Discussion | What is the main finding? (relates to objective) | "we conclude", "our results show" |
| **Implication** | Discussion | What does the conclusion mean in context? (recommendation, applications, future research) | "we recommend", "this implies" |
| **Support** | Results & Discussion | Evidence supporting the main conclusion (own results, references). Often occur in chains. | "figure X shows" |
| **Counterargument** | Discussion | Argues against conclusion or support (errors, methodology problems, contradictions, alternative explanations, limitations) | |
| **Refutation** | Discussion | Refutes a counterargument | "However..." |

## Using SAM for Writing (IMRD Structure)

### INTRODUCTION
- General introduction
- Summary of previous research, ending with the **MOTIVE**
- The **OBJECTIVE** followed by research questions

### METHOD
- Description of materials and procedures, with explanations or references where necessary

### RESULTS
- Each paragraph describes one **SUPPORT** chain

### DISCUSSION
- **OBJECTIVE** is repeated and **MAIN CONCLUSION** is given
- From each **SUPPORT** chain the topmost element is repeated (summary)
- References to previous research as additional **SUPPORTS** (corroboration)
- **COUNTERARGUMENTS** and/or **REFUTATIONS** are given
- The **IMPLICATION** is given

## Key Notes

- Mentioning counterarguments shows critical evaluation ability
- Refuting counterarguments adds persuasive strength
- Be aware of audience — supports obvious to you may not be obvious to readers
- Widely accepted theories need not be explicitly referenced
- Not all scientific texts contain all seven elements
